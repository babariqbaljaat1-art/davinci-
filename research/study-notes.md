## 2. Smart Money Course (8 videos) — what retail/SMC does (IE uses it as the trap)
- 2.3 Structure 1: DEF higher timeframe (HTF) = any TF above the one you are on; HTF is in charge of direction; lower TF only for entries. R: always build narrative from HTF.
- 2.4 Structure 2: DEF bullish = HH/HL, bearish = LH/LL, consolidation = sideways = builds liquidity both sides. DEF BOS = break of the last higher low (bull) / lower high (bear); retail reads it as trend change.
- 2.5 Structure 3: weekly -> daily -> 4h -> 1h -> entry TF must be read together; to make an HTF higher low the LTF must go bearish short-term (counter-trend). R: trade towards the HTF target (e.g. sell on LTF into the weekly higher low).
- 2.6 Imbalance 1: DEF imbalance/FVG = 3-candle pattern, gap between wick of candle 1 and wick of candle 3; impulsive, inefficient delivery; price tends to return to fill it, a partial tap is enough.
- 2.7 Imbalance 2: examples; imbalances get filled partially then later fully; use with structure.
- 2.8 POI/OB 1: DEF POI = HTF area (supply/demand); order block = last candle before the imbalance that broke structure (SMC entry). "We don't trade like this."
- 2.9 POI/OB 2: SMC flow: HTF POI -> LTF BOS -> pullback into LTF order block -> entry. (IE: this is where retail gets in = liquidity for IE.)
- 2.10 OB entries: SMC example (EURUSD daily POI -> 1h BOS -> 15m OB, 3-pip stop, 1:15). Shows tight-stop/high-RR style.

## 3. Inter Equity Course (14 videos)
- 3.11 Liquidity: DEF liquidity = stop losses + pending (limit/stop) orders. Market gravitates to liquidity; if there is not enough it ENGINEERS it (induces traders). "If you don't know the liquidity you become the liquidity." Structure = liquidity, but not every high/low is liquidity.
- 3.12 Retail liquidity: where retail enters and where their stops sit: breakout (buy stop above buildup, stop below it), break & retest (entry on retest, stop below), trendline (3rd touch, stop below last low), SMC (BOS -> pullback to OB, stop below OB/low). R: everyone's stops end up below each low of a trend (above each high in a downtrend) -> the market takes them. R: "We don't trade with structure."
- 3.13 External liquidity: DEF = range/structural highs and lows (stops beyond them). R: once external liquidity is run, anticipate a move back in the opposite direction. Market moves liquidity -> liquidity (not by structure); a sweep of an external low is not automatically a bearish BOS.
- 3.14 Internal liquidity: DEF = liquidity inside the range (between external points). R: once enough internal liquidity is taken on one side, price hunts the external liquidity on the other side. Internal/external depends on timeframe.
- 3.15 Combined: R: external high taken -> expect lower prices into internal lows -> once internal lows swept, buy at those lows targeting the external highs (and vice versa). "Ping-pong" both directions.
- 3.16 The Model (MGM / money glitch model), 3 steps:
  1) BUILDUP of liquidity: early traders induced, one of 4 buildups: trend/channel, breakout, break & retest, SMC BOS/OB. R: "Without a buildup the model is completely invalid." DONT: trade with the structure before the buildup is cleared.
  2) TRAP MOVE clearing the buildup liquidity (their stops).
  3) ENTRY: buys BELOW the lows (sells ABOVE the highs) — at a liquidity block.
  TARGET: the INDUCEMENT HIGH (bull) / LOW (bear) = the high/low printed by the buildup where the trap move started.
- 3.17 Inducement highs/lows (Marco): DEF inducement leg = leg that induced early traders; inducement low = point where the trap move (liquidity run) started. R: after the trap move, sell back into the inducement low (it should get taken). False reactions from "false order blocks" are where retail buys; only another chance to sell. On LTF wait for the same model (false reaction -> trap move -> induce) for the entry. Stop beyond, target liquidity. Fractal: works on every TF.
- 3.18 Inducement from the left: R: find the TRUE draw on liquidity = the area where traders were induced FROM (origin of the leg, e.g. the high sellers sold from; their stops are above it). A sweep of nearby equal highs that does not clear that origin is NOT a liquidity block. DONT sell until the draw on liquidity is swept.
- 3.19 Liquidity blocks: DEF LB = the high/low printed after running a level of liquidity. VALID only if (1) it runs a level of liquidity AND (2) leaves an imbalance OR the next candle closes beyond the previous candle's low (bear) / high (bull). Zone = the candle(s) of the move incl. the extreme. ENTRY options: (a) at the imbalance, (b) 50% of the imbalance, (c) touch of the LB candle.
- 3.20 Liquidity blocks continued: R: NOT every LB is tradable.
  INVALID if (a) "with structure": e.g. bullish market sweeps a low then prints a new higher high -> buying that low on the retrace = trading like retail SMC -> no. It becomes valid only after price builds a counter buildup (lower high, induces, builds liquidity) and takes it out before tapping the LB.
  INVALID if (b) no buyers/sellers trapped right before the tap (needs a trap: early traders' stops taken on the way into the LB).
  CAUTION (c) if there is still liquidity beyond the LB (unswept lows below a bullish LB) the market can sweep it first.
  (d) "If there's no buildup, there is no trade." People who buy every LB become the buildup.
  (e) Needs OPPOSING liquidity to target ("a reason why the market is coming for these highs").
  Checklist for a valid trade: buildup + trap + LB tap + opposing liquidity.
- 3.21 Fractal nature: R: same model on every TF, only the pace differs. Trade the TF that looks cleanest. TFs he uses: weekly (weekly highs/lows), daily (bias), 4h (HTF bias), 1h (intraday), 15m, 5m, 1m. DONT trade the daily (stop too big). R: if the intraday target gives >= 1:3, take it from the 1h LB; take partials at the intraday target (1:3/1:4), hold the rest. If the stop is too big, refine the LB on 15m/5m or take a confirmation entry on 1m. R: "wait until all the liquidity on the downside is gone before I buy." EX US30 1h: sellers induced at a triple top, buyers induced from the left, buyers trapped, tap of 1h LB -> buy, 3.5R to the intraday target.
- 3.22 Entries (diagrams), the step-by-step rule set:
  R1 OPPOSING LIQUIDITY: liquidity built on one side (e.g. a high respected several times). "Wait for liquidity to be taken on the other side, then target the opposing side."
  R2 BUYERS INDUCED: a BOS/move to the upside (retail buys: SMC, S/R, trendline).
  R3 BUYERS TRAPPED: the low below them is taken (their stops) — buildup cleared — into a LIQUIDITY BLOCK right underneath where they got trapped. "We are usually the last people in the market; those trades are the fastest."
  R4 BUILDUP must have happened before the trap.
  ENTRY TYPES: (a) HTF risk entry: "as soon as the low goes", stop below the LB, if RR >= 1:3 to the target; (b) refined LB on 15m/5m; (c) CONFIRMATION entry: the same sequence on the lower TF inside the HTF zone (LTF buyers induced by a minor BOS -> LTF low swept = buyers trapped -> buy below that low, stop below the LTF LB / HTF LB, target the highs).
  DONT take the first 1-minute LB after the HTF low is taken; the market can go as deep as anywhere inside the HTF LB. Wait for more price action / buyers trapped on the LTF.
  INVALIDATION: if after the trap price breaks the internal high (BOS up) without giving the entry, "I'm not allowed to take the trade" until a new buildup forms. "We're all about probability."
  Inducement high is a confluence, not the target; target the opposing liquidity (highs); PARTIALS at an intraday level because after the inducement high is taken a deep pullback can come back to entry.
  OK to widen the stop below the whole LB on LTF entries (stops are small anyway).
- 3.23 Entries continued (chart examples, US30):
  R: "We wait for liquidity to be formed (engineered), then we take the trade. If there's low liquidity, we don't take the trade."
  R: HTF -> pick the LB TF by RR: 4h LB gave < 1:3 to the target -> not allowed; 1h LB gave 3.7-3.8R -> take it, PARTIAL 50-80% at the intraday target, hold the rest.
  R: the LB must have swept the liquidity "from the left" where buyers were first induced (origin of the BOS), not just a nearby low.
  R: if the trap happens but price never taps the 1h LB -> no trade, unless a confirmation entry appears on the LTF.
  R: after a LTF BOS up (buyers induced) you must wait for the low to be taken (buyers trapped) before buying.
  DONT trade around 1 pm New York (US30 has a PM session, still not recommended). Newer case studies = stricter step-by-step entries.
  Sequence: buildup -> run of liquidity -> internal buildup -> induce + trap -> entry, stop below the LB.
- 3.24 Importance of direction bias (Marco): R: HTF first, LTF second. If the HTF (M15/H1/H4) still has liquidity to hunt (e.g. unswept lows below a long idea), a perfect LTF model works briefly and then fails. "Your M5/M1 refinements mean absolutely nothing" if the HTF direction is wrong. R: only trade when confident in the direction; otherwise sit out / live test. Forcing entries -> stop after stop -> you miss the real one.

## 4. Key Components (2 videos)
- 4.25 General rule system (Marco):
  R1 Trading with structure is low probability -> avoid. To short a bullish structure you must short from ABOVE the high (after it's taken), never on the BOS pullback.
  R2 EX: react off a smart-money trap on the left, LB forms, retail shorts the BOS, trap the short sellers above that high into the LB -> sell; target the low that was reacted off twice (buyers sitting there).
  R3 "Identify your liquidity and wait for it to get taken. Stay numb and don't be induced." A sell-off BOS inside a bullish idea is not a buy until the real liquidity (the low under retail's POI) is taken.
  R4 A move from low to high that happens without the liquidity being taken = "not a move we want to be involved in" (prediction, not the system).
  R5 Logic over pattern: a BOS down does not forbid shorts; ask where the liquidity is. "We have to induce higher in order to go lower."
  R6 MGMT: don't hold after the target liquidity is taken ("the liquidity is taken, this buy setup is done"); partial at intermediate liquidity.
- 4.26 Current narrative (Marco): R: follow the narrative that created the setup. If you sell because buyers were induced and liquidity sits below a key low, do NOT look for buys when minor lows are taken; only after THAT low is taken. Entry needs (1) an LB and (2) a buildup; entry when the buildup is taken (spike above the respected highs), stop above the LB high, target resting liquidity on the other side. A second LB + buildup later = confirmation entry. "This is how you avoid losses."

## 16. Trading Psychology (8 videos; 2 are duplicates of 3.11 Liquidity and 3.15 Internal/External)
- 16.1 Core emotional challenges: FOMO (move left without you -> don't chase; wait for the next level), greed (not taking partials), fear of loss (closing early / skipping valid trades after losses), revenge trading (last step before blowing the account), overconfidence (raising risk after a win streak, skipping rules), impatience (entering before the low goes = becoming the early buyer).
- 16.2 Mindset: think in probabilities, no trade is guaranteed; process over money; "less is more" — wait for high-probability setups, you don't need a trade every day.
- 16.3 Emotional regulation: FOMO -> mark the next level, set alerts, walk away. Greed -> take partials; walk away after a big day. After ~3 losing days -> lower risk or take partials earlier to rebuild confidence. "You shouldn't be taking that many trades in one day." In drawdown: stick to the rules, lower risk if needed, one trade can recover.
- 16.4 Practical: daily routine; risk = what you can lose without emotion (he started at $250 on a $200k prop account = 0.125%); journal trades AND emotions; checklist/rules before every entry.
- 16.5 Long-term mastery: when the market is building liquidity / respecting structure, stay away or trade another pair; admit mistakes; don't overtrade; focus on being profitable, not right (partials).
- 16.6 Metrics: growth over perfection; rebuild trust after losses slowly; month/year view.
- Software implications: risk/position-size calculator, max trades per day, loss-streak warning, trade journal with notes.

# RULEBOOK — Inter Equity, compiled from every video above (the engine implements these, ids in [ ])

## A. The setup (what must be on the chart) — any failure = "IE would not take it"
- [A1 trap/LB] A liquidity block = the high (bear) / low (bull) printed by a move that RUNS intact liquidity (an untaken swing high/low). (3.19, 12)
- [A2 displacement] The LB is valid only with an imbalance OR a close beyond the previous candle's low/high right after the sweep. (3.19)
- [A3 buildup] A buildup must exist: BOS (retail induced) + at least one lower high / higher low (early traders in). "Without a buildup the model is completely invalid." (3.16, 3.20d, 12, 13.3)
- [A4 trap] The buildup must be taken (early traders trapped) on the way into the LB — the entry is AFTER the liquidity is taken, never before. "Wait for the low to go." (3.20b, 3.22 R3, 4.25 R3, Dec 2/14, Jan 4/18, Feb 22)
- [A5 opposing liquidity] There must be engineered liquidity on the other side (a respected low/high) to target. "If there is no engineered opposing liquidity, that is already low probability." (3.20e, 3.22 R1, 3.23, 12 S3, Jan 24)
- [A6 not with structure] Not "with structure": a short must come from ABOVE a high that retail sells under; if price already took the opposite low on the left (BOS in the trade direction) and you'd be entering a pullback = retail SMC = no. (3.20a, 4.25 R1, 15.2, Jan 10, Nov 25, Mar 29)
- [A7 inducement from the left] The LB must sweep the TRUE draw — the origin of the induced leg on the left, not just a nearby equal high/low. (3.18, 3.23, Apr 19)
- [A8 RR] At least 1:3 to the FIRST target; otherwise wait deeper or skip ("98% of the time nothing below 1:3"). (3.21, 3.23, Nov 18, Dec 6, Mar 9)
- [A9 liquidity beyond LB] Caution if unswept liquidity still sits just beyond the LB — the market can take it first ("liquidity on both sides -> sit on hands"). (3.20c, Dec 6, Mar 29, Dec 18)
- [A10 stop] Stop always beyond the LB (cover the whole LB, small breathing room); "the size of the LB dictates the stop"; too big -> refine on LTF or skip. (3.22, Jan 4/18, Dec 12)

## B. Direction (bias) — "the most important thing is direction"
- [B1 HTF] Trade only with the HTF narrative (weekly/daily/4h): after HTF buy-side is taken, look for sells into sell-side and vice versa. LTF is irrelevant if the HTF is unclear -> no trade. (2.3, 3.24, Dec 2, Mar 22, Dec 17)
- [B2 daily candles] Longs need a previous-day (or session) low taken first; shorts a previous-day high. "In order for me to take a long I need price to trade below lows." Daily low taken -> target daily high. (Dec 16, Dec 18, 5.#1)
- [B3 extremes] No trades at all-time highs / above HTF highs in "no man's land"; don't hunt HTF extremes counter-bias ("extreme entries on a counter-bias move = predicting"). (Dec 6, Dec 14, Apr 5, Mar 29)
- [B4 no liquidity no bias] After a massive move with no fresh liquidity the bias cannot be determined -> sit out. (Dec 20, Nov 26)

## C. Time (New York clock)
- [C1 session] New York only: on charts 7:30-8:00, entries ~8:00-11:00. Asia/London only HTF limits at reduced risk (not the default). (5, 14.1, Jan 18, Nov 25)
- [C2 news] No entries before 8:30 / 9:45 / 10:00 red news, CPI, NFP, FOMC (wait for the release); not minutes before Powell. FOMC day: NY morning is liquidity building. (13.1, 5.#2, 14.1, Feb 1, Apr 19, Dec 18)
- [C3 indices open] Indices: treat 9:30 NYSE open like a red-folder event -> no entries before 9:30. (Feb 15, Feb 28, Jan 12, Apr 19)
- [C4 trap hour] 9-10 am = trap hour; 10 am (1h/4h closes at 9/10) often sweeps the 9 am move and starts the continuation. (13.2, Dec 21, Jan 18, Apr 19, Dec 12)
- [C5 cut-off] No new trades after ~11:00 NY (London close); "1 pm is not a time to trade"; PM only to hold. Close 80-90% around 11:30. (3.23, 14.1, Feb 15, Jan 26, Mar 15)
- [C6 Friday] Friday after ~11 / late Friday = no trades; Friday is fundamentally driven / reversals. (Nov 22, Jan 18, Jan 26)
- [C7 holidays] No trading on US bank holidays and 24-26 Dec / New Year. (Nov 25, Dec 16, Feb 15, Jan 17)

## D. Day type
- [D1 after a big move] After a big-move day the next day is a range; Monday after a huge Thu/Fri (NFP) move -> sit out. "After such a big move you should not be trading." (Jul 20, Nov 18, Dec 16, Jan 4, Apr 5)

- [D4 liquidity both sides] Clear liquidity on both sides -> wait for one side to be taken first. (Dec 6, Dec 18, Mar 29)

## E. Entry types
- [E1 risk entry] "As soon as the liquidity is taken" at the LB, stop beyond the LB, if >= 1:3 — his main entry. (3.22a, Dec 6, Dec 13, Jan 6)
- [E2 refined] Deeper (the imbalance / the swept level) when E1 gives < 1:3. (3.22b, 5.#1, Mar 9)
- [E3 confirmation] Same model on the LTF inside the HTF LB (LTF buildup -> LTF trap -> LTF LB); never the FIRST 1m LB after the HTF sweep. (3.22c, 13.3)
- [E4 candle close] Close beyond the previous candle after the sweep, then retest (for those who want a rejection). (5.#1, Dec 21, Apr 19)

## F. Management
- [F1 partials] Partial at structural lows/highs and the intraday target (25-50% first, 78-90% by the induced high); hold a runner to the HTF target only when the HTF says so. (3.21, 14.1, Nov 18, Apr 19, Jan 31)
- [F2 roll/BE] After the next structural high/low is taken (the entry became an inducement point) -> roll the stop / break-even. Don't roll too early; don't let live candles change targets. (14.1, Nov 22, Jan 10, Dec 17/18)
- [F3 session close] Close most of it by ~11:30 NY / before the PM session or late Friday. (14.1, Jan 4, Jan 31)
- [F4 adds] Only add after the first position is reduced/protected. (Feb 28, Jan 24)
